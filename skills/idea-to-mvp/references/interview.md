# Recommendation-led founder interview

Read during discovery and whenever new material questions appear. This is a topic map, not a form or a fixed number of rounds.

## Interaction contract

- Ask the questions that change decisions now. Use earlier answers and available project evidence; do not make the founder repeat their brief.
- Usually present 3–5 related questions, with flexible pacing. There is no upper limit on follow-up rounds or total questions. A late contradiction, unchosen consequential rule, weak core output or unknown setup fact is a reason to keep asking.
- For every question, offer your concrete recommended choice and a short explanation of the benefit/tradeoff. Add alternatives only when they clarify a real choice.
- Let the founder answer briefly, accept the full batch, choose exceptions, or propose an alternative. Keep recommendations distinct from adopted decisions.
- After an answer, record the decision and rationale, research new uncertainties, and ask the next useful questions. Briefly recap changed decisions; do not resend the entire brief every round.
- If answers are incomplete, continue independent work and keep the unanswered item pending. Elapsed time and silence are not approval.

Example:

> **What should the customer have accomplished at the end of the first session?**
> **Recommendation:** They finish one usable plan and can export or apply it immediately. That makes the paid value tangible before we add a recurring workflow. Would you change the result or where our responsibility ends?

For an unknown fact, recommend how to obtain/use the correct value rather than fabricating it:

> **Which legal operator should appear on the product?**
> **Recommendation:** Use the exact operator on the payment account. Please supply it or point me to an authorized existing source; I will not infer it from a suggested stack.

“All recommended” accepts actual options in the current batch. If the question sought an unavailable fact, leave it unresolved. Specific corrections supersede general acceptance. If a new answer invalidates a previous rule, explain the conflict and ask which rule should govern.

## Foundation and value

Find the paying customer, concrete problem, situation that triggers use, present workaround, and useful result. Investigate who can be reached initially and why they would pay instead of using a general coding agent, an existing tool, or doing nothing.

Understand founder constraints that affect the plan: launch urgency, available content/assets, operating capacity, relevant expertise and spending limits. Recommend useful milestones but do not repeatedly press for a personal income goal the founder considers irrelevant. Do not treat aspirations as forecasts.

Identify the key differentiator and supporting evidence. If the proposed advantage already exists elsewhere, say so and recommend a sharper outcome. Preserve the founder's business; a major pivot is their choice.

## Complete customer journeys

Walk through discovery/landing → first action → input/setup → useful output → applying that output → payment/access where applicable → returning/learning. Decide where accounts are necessary and where they add friction. Distinguish the user's job from internal architecture.

Ask about concrete outputs, required information, input fallbacks, editing/undo, saving, exporting, errors, retries, empty states and eventual deletion. For recurring products, ask why the customer returns and how the next cycle improves. For collaborative products, resolve ownership, roles, invitations, visibility and moderation.

For AI products, resolve source evidence, user-confirmed facts, permissible generation, review/approval, exact output expectations, revision behavior and meaningful quality evaluation. Show a realistic example before claiming the proposed output is sufficient. Ask which claims, content and external actions require approval.

## Product rules and scope

Resolve rules that change money, access, ranking, credits, participation, time, incentives, privacy or collaboration. Use worked examples to find contradictions. Cover relevant edge cases without enumerating unrelated hypothetical risks.

Discuss what belongs in V1 and what can follow. Recommend a coherent complete journey and explain tradeoffs. If the founder wants all levels/channels/multiplayer/etc., retain that decision and plan the work. Do not call “MVP” permission to shrink approved scope.

Identify user/provider dependencies and responsibility boundaries. Do not promise that possession of one API key completes OAuth, account permissions, provider approval, sender verification, webhooks or prices.

## UX and public experience

Ask for design references and the intended feel. Recommend a clear visual direction and information hierarchy specific to the product. Decide navigation, main action, dashboard content and where advanced detail belongs. Use the fewest steps consistent with the actual task.

Include mobile behavior and accessibility in acceptance. Determine language/market needs rather than copying another founder's launch language. Scope public product pages, pricing, trust/proof, FAQ, support, legal pages, search content and metadata appropriate to the product. Ask for authentic proof and identity instead of inventing testimonials, metrics or legal details.

## Business model after customer value

Settle what is usefully free, what is paid, who pays, and the purchase unit. Recommend subscriptions only when there is a credible recurring job; compare one-time purchases or usage models where suitable.

Research benchmarks, realistic service/AI costs and operating work. Distinguish test prices and estimates from validated willingness to pay. Resolve the relevant limits, fair usage, upgrades, cancellation, rollover/expiry, refunds, disputes and failed-payment access rules. Preserve required customer rights; research jurisdiction-specific requirements when they affect the decision.

Ask how value will be measured: successful useful output, application/activation, repeat use, qualified outcomes or revenue where attributable. Avoid adding vanity dashboards solely because data is available.

## Operational handoff

Inspect tools and existing access first. Then ask only unresolved owner decisions: correct repository and visibility, existing/new cloud target, billing account and spending scope, region/residency, domain/DNS, legal/contact/admin facts, provider readiness and activation boundary. Never ask for secrets in chat.

Discuss whether previews or business validation need additional work before building. Recommend them when they resolve a concrete uncertainty; the founder may skip them. A technical spike that is genuinely needed to establish feasibility should have a bounded purpose and reported outcome.

## Interview readiness

Draft the product document when there is enough coherence; a draft can expose further questions. Planning is ready for build when:

- Core customer journeys and useful outputs are concrete and accepted.
- Scope, material product/business rules and UX direction have no unresolved contradictions.
- Important dependencies, owner decisions, acceptance criteria and deferrals are explicit.
- The founder can review the proposed result and choose whether to build.

Mark nonblocking uncertainties honestly and assign the next action. Keep asking about material gaps at any point. Do not declare the interview finished merely because these headings were visited or a recommended batch was accepted.
